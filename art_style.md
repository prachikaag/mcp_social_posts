# Miraya Art Style Guide
## "Woh Har Roz Ki Ladki" — The Everyday Woman

**This file is read by every design-generation agent and skill before creating any visual.**
All images, carousels, and story posts must conform to these guidelines.

---

## Core Visual Philosophy

Miraya's visual identity is built on **emotional truth, not aspiration fantasy.**

We do not show perfect models in perfect settings.
We show **real women — the kind who wake up at 5am, pack tiffins, manage meetings, help with homework, and still find a moment to feel beautiful.**

Every image should make our audience think: *"That's me. I see myself in this."*

---

## Primary Visual Style: Warm Caricature Illustration

**Style name:** *Desi Glow* — Warm watercolour caricature with comic storytelling elements

**Visual references to aim for:**
- Amul ads: bold outlines, expressive faces, instantly relatable situations
- Modern Indian illustration (Priya's Shakti, Bhumika Sharma illustration style)
- NOT: Western cartoon, anime, or corporate flat design

**Key visual characteristics:**
- **Line work:** Bold, confident outlines (2–3px) — hand-drawn feel, not perfectly geometric
- **Colour fill:** Warm watercolour wash inside outlines — slightly textured, not flat digital fills
- **Expressions:** Large, expressive eyes. Exaggerated but loving facial expressions. NO tiny expressionless faces.
- **Body types:** Curvy, real, diverse — different ages (20s to 40s), different builds, different skin tones. Never thin-only.
- **Skin tones:** Full range of Indian skin tones — from fair to deep brown. Celebrate all.
- **Hair:** Varied — tied in a bun (busy mum), open and flowing (her moment), dupatta-covered, braid. Show real hair, not always perfect.
- **Clothing in story panels:** Plain, everyday clothes BEFORE the Miraya reveal. Vivid, glowing ethnic wear AFTER.

---

## Carousel Cohesion Rules *(Applied to every multi-slide post)*

**Rule 1 — One palette per carousel. No exceptions.**
Every slide in a carousel must share the same colour palette.
Choose ONE palette from the approved list below before generating any slide.
All backgrounds, text colours, accent elements must come from this palette only.

**Rule 2 — Consistent character design.**
If a character appears across multiple slides, she must look the same — same hair, same face shape, same skin tone. She is the same woman throughout.

**Rule 3 — Consistent typography.**
Use ONE headline font and ONE body font across all slides.
Recommended pairing: Bold rounded sans-serif (headline) + clean readable sans (body)

**Rule 4 — Consistent illustration style.**
If slide 1 uses caricature illustration, ALL slides use caricature illustration.
Do not mix photography and illustration in the same carousel.

**Rule 5 — Visual breathing room.**
Leave consistent padding/margin across all slides (minimum 48px from edges).
Avoid clutter — each slide should communicate ONE idea, visually.

## Story Post Structure: "Woh Har Roz Ki Ladki" Series

This is Miraya's core storytelling format. Each post is a 4–6 panel comic strip
showing one chapter of an everyday Indian woman's life — and how Miraya fits into it.

### The Emotional Arc (same in every story post)
```
Panel 1–3: Her reality — she carries so much, so quietly.
Panel 4:   A small moment that's just hers.
Panel 5:   She wears Miraya. Something shifts.
Panel 6:   She glows — not because she changed. Because she remembered her worth.
```

### Panel Layout
- Square format (1:1) for Instagram feed
- 4–6 panels per post, arranged in a 2-column grid within the square
- OR: Carousel format where each panel = one slide (deeper emotional journey)
- Bold panel borders — like a comic book page
- Panel borders use the Primary Accent colour from the chosen palette

### Panel Text Rules
- **Narration boxes:** Top or bottom of panel, Primary Accent background, white text, 12–14px
- **Speech bubbles:** Hand-drawn style, slight imperfection. Hinglish text inside.
- **Emotion captions:** Between panels or below final panel. Larger, serif font. This is the "feels" line.
- **Max text per panel:** 15 words. Let the illustration carry the emotion, not the words.

### Character Design for Story Posts

**Her name (internal reference):** She doesn't have a fixed name — she is every woman.

**Visual character archetypes:**

*Archetype A — The Young Mother (late 20s)*
- Hair in a messy bun, bindi, slightly tired eyes but warm smile
- Usually seen with a toddler clinging to her dupatta
- Wears plain salwar kameez in panels 1–3, Miraya kurta in panels 4–6

*Archetype B — The Working Woman (early 30s)*
- Professional yet slightly frazzled — dupatta half-tucked, bag over one shoulder
- Seen rushing between office and home
- Wears formal clothes in panels 1–3, Miraya in her "me time" panels

*Archetype C — The Elder Sister / Beta Beti (20s)*
- Helps parents, studies or works, manages siblings
- Quiet strength, responsible beyond her years
- Wears casual clothes, transforms in Miraya for a family occasion or festival

*Archetype D — The Woman of the House (late 30s–40s)*
- The one everyone depends on. Always last to eat, last to sleep.
- Warm, full of love, usually shown in the kitchen or managing the household
- Her Miraya moment is deeply earned — feels like a standing ovation

## Caption Style for Story Posts

**Structure:**
```
Line 1: A question or observation that immediately resonates (Hinglish)
Line 2–4: The story in 3–4 short Hinglish lines — mirror her reality
Line 5: The turn — the moment she chooses herself
Final line: The brand line — short, emotional, definitive
CTA: DM or store visit (never a link)
```

**Emotional tone:** Celebratory, not pitying. She is not a victim — she is extraordinary.
The caption says: *"We see you. And you deserve this."*

---

## What to Never Do

- Never show a thin, model-perfect woman as the "hero" of a story post
- Never use the word "deserve" in a guilty/sad way — use it as celebration
- Never make her look overwhelmed to the point of breakdown — strong, not suffering
- Never use generic stock photo aesthetic — every image must feel hand-crafted
- Never mix more than one colour palette in a single carousel
- Never use white backgrounds in story posts — always use palette background colour
- Never skip the final "glow" panel — every story must end with her shining

## Image Generation Prompt Template

When generating images for Miraya story posts, always start with this base prompt
and append the specific scene description:

```
BASE PROMPT (always include):
"Warm watercolour caricature illustration in the Amul/Desi Glow style.
Bold hand-drawn outlines, watercolour wash fills. Expressive, loving caricature
of an Indian woman [age range]. Real body type, [skin tone] complexion.
Warm, celebratory mood. Colour palette: [PALETTE NAME AND HEX CODES].
Comic panel format with narration box. Hinglish text overlay.
Style: Indian illustration, NOT anime, NOT western cartoon, NOT photorealistic."

THEN APPEND: [specific scene description from the story panel]
```
